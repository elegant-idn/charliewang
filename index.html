About Me

I am a senior fullstack developer who enjoys the whole journey of building for the web: understanding the problem, shaping the architecture, writing the code and making sure it runs smoothly in production.

My work sits where business goals meet engineering. Before I write a single line of code, I want to understand who will use the product, what they are trying to achieve and what success looks like for the people paying for it. That context drives every technical decision I make, from choosing a database to deciding how a form should behave when the network drops.

On the backend, I build APIs and services that are well structured, well tested and easy for other developers to pick up. I pay close attention to data modelling, security, performance under load and clear boundaries between parts of the system, because those are the things that become expensive to fix later.

On the frontend, I build interfaces that feel quick and predictable. I treat accessibility, responsive layouts and loading performance as part of the job rather than extras, and I work closely with designers to turn their ideas into components that stay consistent across the whole product.

As a senior engineer, I also spend a lot of my time making teams better. I review code with care, write documentation people actually read, mentor less experienced developers and help set up the tooling, pipelines and conventions that let a team move fast without breaking things.

I am comfortable joining an existing codebase and improving it step by step, and equally comfortable starting from an empty repository. Legacy systems do not scare me. Some of my most satisfying work has been taking an old, fragile application and turning it into something the team is proud to maintain.

Outside of client work, I keep learning. I follow the web platform closely, experiment with new tools in side projects and only bring them into production once I understand their trade-offs. Stability for my clients always comes before novelty for me.

How I Work

A good process removes surprises. Here is what working together usually looks like, adjusted to the size and pace of each project.

1. Discovery

We start with a conversation about your goals, users, constraints and deadlines. I ask a lot of questions, review any existing code or designs and make sure we agree on what problem we are actually solving before any money is spent on building.

2. Planning and Architecture

I turn what we learned into a clear plan: the main features, the technical approach, the risks and a realistic estimate. You get a written outline you can share with your team, so everyone knows what will be built and why.

3. Iterative Development

I build in short cycles and deliver working software early. You can see progress on a staging environment, give feedback as we go and change priorities when the business needs it, instead of waiting months for a big reveal.

4. Testing and Quality

Automated tests, code reviews and manual checks are part of every cycle, not a phase at the end. I test across browsers and devices and look closely at performance, security and accessibility before anything goes live.

5. Launch and Support

Deployment is planned and calm. After launch, I monitor the application, fix issues quickly and hand over documentation so your team can maintain it. If you need ongoing development, I am happy to stay on for the long term.

What You Can Expect

Skills matter, but so does the way someone works with you. These are the principles I bring to every project.

Honest Communication

I tell you what is going well and what is not. If a deadline is at risk or an idea will cost more than it is worth, you will hear it from me early, along with options to move forward.

Ownership

I treat your product as if it were my own. That means thinking about edge cases, following up on loose ends and not considering a task finished until it works properly in production.

Quality Without Over-Engineering

I aim for the simplest solution that solves the real problem well. Clean code and good tests, yes. Complexity for its own sake, no.

Security by Default

Input validation, safe authentication, careful handling of personal data and regular dependency updates are built into my everyday work, not added as an afterthought.

Performance Focus

Fast applications keep users happy and convert better. I measure, find real bottlenecks and fix them, both in the browser and on the server.

Knowledge Sharing

I document decisions, write clear handover notes and explain my work so your team is never dependent on a single person to keep the product running.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions I hear most often from new clients.

Do you work remotely or on site?

Mostly remotely, from Lodz, Poland. I work comfortably with teams across different time zones and keep a predictable schedule for meetings and updates. For clients in or near Lodz, on-site workshops or kickoff meetings can be arranged when they add value.

What size of projects do you take on?

Anything from a focused task, like fixing performance problems or adding a feature, to building a complete product from scratch. I also join existing teams as a senior engineer for longer engagements.

Can you work with my existing codebase?

Yes. I regularly join projects that are already running. I start by reading the code, understanding how it is deployed and identifying the biggest risks, then improve things gradually while continuing to deliver new features.

How do you estimate time and cost?

After a short discovery phase, I break the work into clear pieces and estimate each one. You get a realistic range rather than an optimistic single number, and I keep you updated if anything changes along the way.

Do you provide support after launch?

Yes. I offer ongoing maintenance, monitoring, bug fixing and feature development after release. If you prefer your own team to take over, I provide a thorough handover with documentation and walkthrough sessions.

Which languages do you speak?

I work in English with international clients and can adapt to the communication style and tools your team already uses, whether that is Slack, Teams, Jira, Linear or something else.
